## LinkedIn

BioMed AI Watch, Oct 18–25: benchmarks vs real clinical inputs.

In Science, an AI system that simulates a panel of physicians reached 83.2% accuracy on NEJM clinicopathological cases, compared with 36.1% for generalist physicians. The authors stress this was a controlled benchmark.

Unreviewed preprints this week show what changes outside that setting. Multi-agent LLM teams improved diagnostic recall on curated vignettes but reversed on 364 real emergency department notes. Four multimodal LLMs changed their glioma-vs-abscess calls based on how the referral was worded, far more than based on the MRI.

On oversight, a randomized paediatric discharge study (also a preprint) measured what clinicians caught in LLM drafts. They removed 18 of 22 unsupported additions but only 6 of 22 incorrect abbreviation expansions.

The gap: diagnostic AI needs prospective testing on live clinical inputs, and human review needs metrics broken down by error type.
